Mona Lisa Hidden Message in Frame

Brooklyn wasn’t looking at the Mona Lisa’s smile; she was staring at a tiny scrap of yellowed paper clipped behind the frame, so well‑hidden it felt impossible that no one else had noticed it. When the crowd shifted, she caught a glimpse of hurried handwriting on the fragile note, the kind that suggested urgency rather than care. She leaned in just enough to confirm it was real, her pulse quickening as she realised someone had deliberately left it there. Stepping back, she scanned the room — tourists, guards, cameras — yet she felt it instantly: someone else had seen her spot the note, and whatever message it carried wasn’t meant to stay hidden much longer.
Brooklyn hesitated, her mind racing. She could take a quick photo — fast and quiet — or she could edge closer and try to write the message down before anyone noticed. Both options felt risky. A photo might draw attention if someone was watching, but getting closer meant standing right beside the world’s most guarded painting. She hovered between the choices, pulse thudding, knowing she had only seconds to decide… and that someone in the room was already paying far too much attention to her.
