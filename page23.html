France

Brooklyn had always known her uncle was a little… unconventional. He collected secrets the way other people collected postcards, and he had a habit of glancing over his shoulder even in the safest places. But she never imagined she’d be sprinting after him through the narrow streets of Paris, her backpack thumping against her spine, while two men in dark coats pushed through the crowd behind them. Julien didn’t shout instructions. He simply looked back once, eyes sharp, and jerked his head for her to follow. So she did. They wove through a market, dodged a flower cart, and burst out onto the Champ de Mars. The Eiffel Tower rose above them like a steel giant, and her uncle made straight for it. Brooklyn’s lungs burned, but adrenaline kept her feet moving. Whatever trouble he was in, she wasn’t letting him face it alone. By the time they reached the second-level lookout, the city stretched beneath them in a glittering sprawl. Her uncle leaned against the railing, catching his breath. The men chasing them were nowhere in sight—either they’d lost interest or they’d lost the trail. Brooklyn stepped beside him, the wind tugging at her hair. “Are you going to tell me what that was about?” Julien gave a tired smile. “Later. For now… look.” Paris shimmered in the afternoon light. From their height, the Seine curled like a ribbon through the city. To the west, the Arc de Triomphe stood proud at the center of its starburst of avenues. To the east, the Louvre stretched long and elegant, its glass pyramid catching the sun. Brooklyn exhaled slowly. The chase was over—for now—and the city felt full of possibility again. “So,” he said, nudging her shoulder, “what next? The Louvre? Or the Arc?” Brooklyn considered the two landmarks, each promising its own kind of adventure. The Louvre with its endless halls and quiet mysteries. The Arc with its sweeping view of the city and the promise of open sky. She smiled, feeling the thrill of choice, of freedom, of a story still unfolding. “Let’s decide on the way down,” she said. “After all that running, I think we’ve earned a little wandering.” Julien laughed softly, and together they turned toward the stairs, Paris waiting below like a map of possibilities.
