Police Caught You

Brooklyn’s pulse hammered as she slipped her hand through the half‑open car window, fingers closing around the worn leather notebook she’d been watching for days. The street was quiet, the kind of stillness that made every sound feel louder, sharper. For a moment, she thought she’d gotten away with it—until a porch light snapped on and a voice shouted her name. The notebook felt heavier than she expected as she ran, as if it already knew the trouble it was about to bring.
Hours later, Brooklyn sat in a stark immigration office, the fluorescent lights buzzing like angry insects above her. The officers spoke in calm, practiced tones, but their words cut deeper than any accusation: she was being deported. Her hands trembled in her lap as she tried to process it—leaving behind her school, her friends, the only place that had ever felt like home. The world outside the window kept moving, but for her, everything had stopped.
