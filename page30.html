Banff

Brooklyn’s breath fogged in the cold mountain air as she arrived in Banff, her pulse quickening with the memory of what she’d seen—her uncle deep in conversation with a group of men dressed for skiing, their laughter too forced, their glances too sharp. The postcard beauty of the snow‑covered peaks did little to calm her nerves. She had followed him across continents, from the heat of Rio to the frozen silence of the Canadian Rockies, and now every instinct told her something wasn’t right. The men’s sleek gear and expensive watches didn’t match the easy camaraderie they pretended to share. Brooklyn tightened her scarf, her eyes scanning the slopes for any sign of her uncle’s familiar silhouette.




When she finally spotted the group gliding down the mountain, she didn’t hesitate. Strapping on her skis, she pushed off, the crisp snow crunching beneath her boots before giving way to smooth speed. The wind whipped past her face as she chased the suspicious men, her focus narrowing to the rhythmic motion of her descent. They carved through the powder with practiced precision, but Brooklyn was faster—driven by fear and determination. Each turn brought her closer, the distance shrinking as the truth loomed ahead like the jagged peaks surrounding her.
