The Grab

Brooklyn steadied her breath as she climbed the jagged rock face inside the cavern, her fingers gripping the cold stone while echoes of dripping water filled the air. The dim glow from her headlamp cast shifting shadows across the walls, revealing ancient markings and narrow ledges. She was close—she could feel it. Her uncle’s trail had led her here, deep beneath the earth, and every scrape on her palms felt like progress. But just as she reached for the next hold, a hand appeared from above. For a split second she thought it was help—until the stranger’s fingers closed not around her wrist, but around her uncle’s leather‑bound notebook tucked into her jacket. In one swift motion, they yanked it free and disappeared into the darkness above.
Brooklyn hauled herself to the top, heart pounding, just in time to see the thief sprinting toward the cavern exit. Outside, two paths waited for her: an old dirt bike leaning against a boulder and a taxi idling as if expecting a passenger. The notebook was the last thing her uncle had left behind, the only clue to where he had gone and why he vanished. She felt the weight of the decision settle on her shoulders—speed and danger on the bike, or strategy and pursuit in the taxi. The cavern wind brushed past her as she hesitated, wondering which path would bring her closer to the truth. Should she chase them on the bike or follow in the taxi?
