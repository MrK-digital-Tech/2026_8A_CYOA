Police don't like meddling children who can't speak French

Brooklyn sat stiffly in the chair across from the officer, her fingers twisting together in her lap as she tried to explain herself. She kept insisting she had only been taking a normal tourist photo of the Mona Lisa, but the officer already knew about the hidden markings she had zoomed in on inside the frame. Every time he asked her another question in rapid French, panic flashed across her face. She could barely understand him, let alone answer properly. Her broken attempts at French came out tangled and nervous, mixing English words with short phrases she barely remembered. The officer’s expression hardened more each time she failed to respond clearly, and the room felt smaller with every passing second.
The officer finally pushed his chair back with a sharp sigh and began speaking more firmly, pointing toward a stack of paperwork on his desk. Brooklyn caught only fragments — “aéroport,” “retour,” and “Toowoomba” — enough to understand he was talking about sending her home. Her stomach dropped. She tried again to explain that she hadn’t meant to cause trouble, that she was only curious about the strange message hidden in the frame, but the words wouldn’t come. The language barrier wrapped around her like a wall she couldn’t climb over, leaving her trapped in silence while the officer discussed arrangements to escort her to the airport before things became even more serious.
