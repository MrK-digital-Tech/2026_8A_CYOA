Brooklyn stood frozen as the ground trembled beneath her feet, the air thick with ash and heat. The volcano that had loomed quietly in the distance was now alive, roaring with fury as molten lava spilled down its sides. The sky turned a haunting shade of orange, and the smell of smoke filled her lungs. Panic surged through her chest, but instinct took over—she turned and ran, her sneakers pounding against the dusty road as the world behind her seemed to crack open. Every step felt heavier, every breath sharper, but she didn’t dare stop.
When she reached the main road, her eyes caught the flash of red and blue lights cutting through the haze. A policeman was directing people away from the danger, his voice barely audible over the rumble of the eruption. Brooklyn waved frantically, shouting for help, her voice hoarse from the smoke. The officer turned toward her, his expression shifting from confusion to urgency as he saw the fear in her eyes. In that chaotic moment, surrounded by ash and sirens, Brooklyn felt both terrified and strangely brave— like she was standing at the edge of something far bigger than herself.
