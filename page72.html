Brooklyn had never seen a sunrise like this one — a thin gold line stretching across the horizon, turning the desert sky into a watercolor of pink and orange. It should’ve been beautiful. But all she felt was the pounding of her heart. She stood at the edge of the river, backpack slung over one shoulder, sneakers soaked from the night’s crossing. Behind her lay everything she knew. Ahead of her lay the United States — a place she’d only seen in movies and in the stories her mother used to tell before she passed.



Brooklyn didn’t have a visa. She didn’t have papers. She barely had a plan. What she did have was a promise she’d made to herself: I’m going to build a life where I’m not afraid all the time. As she climbed the riverbank, she spotted a girl around her age sitting on a rock, hugging her knees. The girl looked up, startled, then relaxed when she saw Brooklyn.
