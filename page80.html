The Throw Up

Brooklyn had been feeling off all morning, but she tried to push through it the way she always did. By midday, the nausea hit her so hard she barely made it to the bathroom before throwing up, her body trembling as she leaned over the sink. She tried to steady herself, but the room spun, and every breath felt heavier than the last. When she collapsed to her knees, clutching her stomach, the fear finally sank in. A neighbour heard her retching and rushed in, helping her into the car and driving straight to the hospital, where fluorescent lights and hurried footsteps swallowed her whole.
Now she lay in a hospital bed, pale and exhausted, listening to the steady beep of the monitor beside her. Nurses moved around her with calm efficiency, but Brooklyn felt small beneath the weight of everything happening at once. She stared at the ceiling, replaying the moment her body gave out, wondering how things had spiraled so quickly. The sadness crept in quietly, settling beside her like an unwelcome visitor. She wasn’t sure what the doctors would say next, but she knew one thing—this was a moment she couldn’t outrun.
